# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Arabic-first personal screen-time tool. The user tracks how they spend time on their Linux desktop (apps, sites, categories), sets daily limits, and gets warned when a limit is exceeded. Used daily, in short glances, from the tray and from a resizable window (min 900x600, default 1280x800). RTL primary, with English support.

## Product Purpose

Wisp watches the desktop quietly and reports honestly: where time went, by day/week/month, per app or category, with configurable limits and gentle notifications when a limit is passed. Success looks like an app you glance at 3-4 times a day, that answers "where did my time go?" in under five seconds, and that warns without nagging.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, friendly (دافئ، مرحّب، ودود). Rose family colors, soft warm surfaces, pill-shaped controls, rounded corners, gentle rose-tinted shadows. The tone of a friendly reminder, not a supervisor.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards: centered hero branding, gray-blue palettes, template card grids, corporate minimalism.
- System-monitor clutter: widget-everywhere density, technical overload, Activity-monitor styling.
- Anything that reads "webapp" in a desktop shell: centered wordmark heroes, no persistent chrome, mouse-only paths.

## Design Principles

- Desktop-native chrome: the content header is a working toolbar, not a hero. Identity lives in the titlebar; the bar earns its height with navigation and actions.
- Glanceable honesty: numbers and labels readable in seconds; hierarchy through scale and color, not density.
- Persistent access: settings and navigation reachable from every view, with a keyboard path (Ctrl+, for settings).
- Gentle by default: warning, not nagging; the brand is a friendly wisp, not a monitor.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text, full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, prefers-reduced-motion respected (already implemented globally).
